## Portfolio purpose

The comprehensive portfolio evaluates whether the candidate demonstrates the knowledge, dispositions, and practices needed to realize the Warner School mission: to transform lives and make the world more just and humane through teaching.

The portfolio requires:

1. a personal statement explaining aims as a teacher, conceptions of good teaching, theory/research framework, and experiences that shaped those commitments;
2. narratives explaining how knowledge, dispositions, and practices align with ten program proficiencies;
3. exhibits from coursework, clinical work, and other relevant educational/professional experiences.

## Common narrative dimensions

Each proficiency narrative is evaluated through:

- **Understandings** - knowledge and reasoning relative to the proficiency;
- **Commitments** - values, ethics, beliefs, and attitudes;
- **Practices** - professional decisions, actions, and interactions.

## Required exhibits

- Lesson plan, materials, and assessment rubric
- Innovative unit, materials, and assessment rubric
- Field experience evaluations
- Student teaching evaluations: CPAST1 and CPAST2
- edTPA portfolio
- NYSED Content Specialty Examination results
- Content preparation review worksheet, if applicable

## Strongly recommended exhibits

- NYSED Educating All Students exam results
- Key papers, research projects, and coursework artifacts
- Lesson and unit plans developed and enacted during clinical experiences
- Samples of student work and assessment data from clinical experiences
- Videos, photos, and other evidence of practice from clinical experiences
- Feedback from advisors, supervisors, cooperating teachers, and students
- Relevant correspondence with school/community members
- Journals, field notes, observations, analyses, and reflections
- Artifacts from other professional learning experiences

## Proficiency 2: Learner Diversity and Equity

The candidate draws from theory, research, and clinical practice to demonstrate commitments to educational equity and justice. The candidate understands how family/community background, race, ethnicity, linguistic and cultural resources, gender and sexual orientation, socioeconomic status, exceptional needs and abilities, and other experiences affect learning. The candidate also understands how social structures and educational institutions affect diverse learners' opportunities and experiences.

### 2.1 Understanding of learner diversity and equity

Show understanding of how personal/cultural characteristics and social/institutional structures shape educational identities, experiences, and needs. Define educational equity and how it is cultivated.

### 2.2 Commitment to cultivating learner diversity and equity

Show respect for learners' diverse backgrounds, interests, and perspectives. Show commitment to equity and justice, especially for systemically marginalized and underserved learners. Explain how this commitment developed and how it will be acted on in the field.

### 2.3 Inclusive, anti-oppressive learning experiences

Show how students' diverse knowledge, experiences, and ways of learning are incorporated into instruction. Demonstrate learning experiences that support pluralism and oppose cultural supremacy.
