# Proficiency 10 — Advocacy and Ethical Leadership

## Claim

I understand advocacy and ethical leadership as part of the teacher’s role, especially in contexts where students are affected by disability, inequity, exclusion, technological power, and institutional decisions made without their voices at the center.

## Leadership Beyond Position

Ethical leadership does not require formal authority. It begins with how a teacher interprets responsibility. I want to be the kind of educator who notices where systems are failing students and acts — through curriculum, communication, policy awareness, and professional stance — to widen dignity, access, and justice. My work in disability studies, AI ethics, and inclusive teaching has made this proficiency especially important to me because I am drawn to the places where institutional harm is often normalized as inevitability.

My coursework and research repeatedly return to the idea that technologies and educational systems are never neutral. Whether the issue is disability support, language access, trauma-informed practice, or AI use in schools, leadership requires asking who benefits, who is burdened, and what alternatives are possible. Advocacy means moving beyond private concern into professional action.

## Ethical Leadership in Computer Science Education

As a future computer science teacher, I see advocacy as inseparable from content. Students need teachers who can help them question algorithmic bias, privacy risks, authorship issues, and the social consequences of digital systems. The AI PD project and my broader interdisciplinary work in data science and equity have prepared me to think critically about these issues in ways that can be translated to K-12 classrooms.

Disability advocacy is equally central to my leadership stance. My EDF447 and inclusive education work have reinforced that students with disabilities and neurodivergent learners do not need pity; they need environments, instruction, and institutional commitments that recognize their humanity and competence. Ethical leadership means challenging deficit narratives and advocating for structures that make access real.
