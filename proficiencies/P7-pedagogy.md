# Proficiency 7 — Pedagogy

## Claim

I use pedagogy to create learning experiences that are active, reflective, inclusive, and intellectually meaningful, with particular attention to how instructional methods support agency, access, and deeper understanding.

## Pedagogy as Choice-Making

Pedagogy is the bridge between planning and lived classroom experience. It is where a teacher’s values become visible in method. My growth in pedagogy has come through designing lessons and professional learning experiences that ask not only what students should learn, but how they should encounter that learning. Across my coursework, I have become increasingly committed to pedagogies that position students as thinkers, makers, collaborators, and critics rather than passive recipients of information.

The AI professional development module is a strong example of this. The core routine — Ask, Check, Choose, Change, Explain — was designed as an instructional scaffold that keeps students active in judgment rather than passive in tool use. Instead of treating AI as an answer machine, the pedagogy frames AI as a provisional learning partner whose outputs must be evaluated, revised, and explained. That pedagogical stance protects student authorship while also cultivating metacognition.

## Active, Inclusive, and Ethical Methods

My pedagogy in computer science is also shaped by project-based and inquiry-centered approaches. Robotics, coding, Minecraft Education, and collaborative problem solving all invite students to learn through doing. However, I have also learned that activity alone is not sufficient. Good pedagogy requires structure, reflection, and accessibility. Students need guided entry points, language supports, visible routines, and opportunities to process what they are learning.

Pedagogically, I am especially committed to methods that help students connect technical work with identity, voice, and ethics. Technology education should not ask students to become less human in order to become more “technical.” I want pedagogy to hold rigor and humanity together.
