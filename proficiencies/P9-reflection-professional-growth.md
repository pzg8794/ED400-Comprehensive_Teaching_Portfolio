# Proficiency 9 — Reflection and Professional Growth

## Claim

I engage in reflection as an essential part of professional growth, using writing, feedback, observation, and self-study to examine my practice, surface my assumptions, and revise my teaching identity over time.

## Reflection as Method

Reflection has been one of the defining practices of my teacher preparation experience. Across ED400A, EDU442, and ED452B, I have repeatedly returned to writing as a way to make sense of what I am seeing, what I am carrying into the classroom, and what kind of teacher I am becoming. Reflection, for me, is not simply retrospective. It is a method of inquiry. It helps me connect lived experience to theory, trace patterns across placements, and confront biases that might otherwise remain invisible.

The Bias Deconstruct paper is a particularly important example because it required me to analyze not only students and institutions, but myself. The paper asked me to name the reflexes I carry into classrooms, especially around neurodivergence, regulation, and language difference. Similarly, the trauma-informed reflection and the reflective portion of the ED452B unit plan portfolio made clear that growth often happens through discomfort, especially when revision forces me to see where my first instincts were incomplete.

## Growth Through Revision

Professional growth is rarely dramatic. More often, it appears in the accumulation of better questions: Why did I interpret that student that way? What did my lesson assume about readiness? Whose participation did my structure make easier, and whose did it make harder? What beliefs about learning are embedded in my response? Over time, reflection has made me slower to judge and quicker to investigate.

I also see reflection as a form of accountability. If I want to teach ethically and inclusively, I need habits that keep me from becoming comfortable with unexamined routines. Reflection helps me stay open to feedback and committed to change.

## Reflection

This proficiency may be the one that most clearly links all the others together. Reflection is what allows knowledge, planning, equity, and pedagogy to become more than isolated competencies. It is how I continue becoming a more deliberate and humane educator.
