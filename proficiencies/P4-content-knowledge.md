# Proficiency 4 — Content Knowledge and Application

## Claim

I demonstrate content knowledge in computer science by connecting disciplinary understanding to age-appropriate pedagogy, cross-curricular integration, and ethical reflection on how technology shapes students’ lives.

## Knowing the Discipline and Teaching It Well

As a teacher candidate in K-12 Computer Science with a strong technical background in computer science, engineering, and data science, I bring substantial disciplinary knowledge into the classroom. However, this proficiency is not only about knowing content. It is about transforming content into learning experiences that are meaningful and accessible for children. My EDE498C and EDU442 work helped me translate abstract computational concepts into forms that young learners could enter through robotics, block-based coding, AI literacy, and project-based design.

For example, the Minecraft unit plan and robotics-based lessons required me to think about algorithms, sequencing, debugging, systems thinking, and digital problem solving not as college-level abstractions but as habits of mind children can practice through concrete experience. Similarly, the AI professional development module required me to frame artificial intelligence as both a technical and ethical topic, helping teachers see that AI literacy involves authorship, privacy, bias, and responsible use in addition to functionality.

## Application Across Contexts

I am particularly interested in computer science as a cross-curricular field. Integrating CS across the curriculum allows students to encounter computing as a tool for communication, creativity, inquiry, and critique rather than as an isolated technical subject. My coursework in EDE498C emphasized this by linking CS standards to classroom learning in ways that felt developmentally appropriate and instructionally purposeful.

My background in data science and AI ethics also strengthens this proficiency. I want students to develop not only technical fluency, but critical awareness of how digital systems work and whom they serve. Teaching content knowledge in CS therefore means helping students understand both how to build with technology and how to think responsibly about its consequences.

## Reflection

This proficiency reflects one of my strongest areas, but it has also challenged me to become more pedagogically precise. Knowing a discipline deeply is not enough; I need to know how to invite children into it without overwhelming them. My goal is to teach computer science as rigorous, creative, and socially meaningful.
