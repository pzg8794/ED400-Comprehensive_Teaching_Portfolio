# Public Copy: Grade 4 IGNITE Minecraft Evidence Summary

Source provenance: private `teaching-placement` repo, especially Grade 4 Baris/Fowler/Schrank/Autore Minecraft transcripts, lesson packets, and evidence notes.

This public copy summarizes teaching evidence without exposing student-identifying details.

## Evidence pattern

The Grade 4 Minecraft Education lessons showed that a high-interest digital tool can expand participation, but only when the teacher designs access routes carefully. Students were excited by Minecraft and team building, yet the classroom evidence also showed repeated access demands:

- signing into Minecraft Education correctly;
- avoiding account lockout through guided procedures;
- locating and entering the correct world;
- using host/join world procedures;
- reading or listening to multi-step directions;
- managing keyboard/mouse and camera controls;
- working with peers at a reasonable noise level;
- asking for help rather than abandoning the task;
- transitioning and cleaning up devices responsibly.

The teaching moves included projected modeling, step-by-step directions, reminders not to enter credentials without support, circulating help, table-by-table movement, explicit noise expectations, and repeated invitations to raise a hand for help.

## Equity interpretation

This evidence matters for Proficiency 2 because it shows why task completion alone is not a fair measure of learning. A student who does not complete a Minecraft task may be struggling with sign-in, interface load, language load, peer coordination, working memory, frustration, or unclear routines rather than the computational-thinking concept itself.

For equitable instruction, the teacher needs to separate access barriers from conceptual barriers. Supports such as projected click paths, read-aloud directions, partner roles, planning sheets, and debugging routines make the learning goal more visible.
