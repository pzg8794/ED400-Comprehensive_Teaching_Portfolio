## Core excerpt

I am preparing to finish the M.S. in Computer Science with inclusion certification and enter my first full year of teaching. I understand teaching as work inside a complex system: many variables, many unknowns, and a deep sense that this is the right work.

I am excited to teach in a community and to try practices I have been building: culturally and linguistically sustaining computer science lessons, trauma-informed and neurodiversity-affirming routines, and technology/AI use that lowers barriers instead of raising them. I am also excited to see what happens when teacher agency as algorithmic justice meets real constraints and real children.

I am concerned about being swallowed by the system I want to help change: pacing guides that do not fit the students in front of me, testing pressure, limited prep time, and policies that use the word equity while rewarding compliance. I want to keep questioning in ways that are sustainable, not burn out into survival mode.

The commitments I want to hold onto are clear:

- Every student belongs; there is no normal child as the standard.
- Relationship comes before content, especially for CLD and neurodivergent students who are often misread.
- Rigor and care are not opposites.
- Technology and AI are tools, not masters; they must be transparent, inclusive, and just.

I feel confident about designing tech-rich learning, spotting patterns in systems that do not make sense, and building lessons with purpose. I want to grow in pacing, boundaries, and legal/policy fluency so I can protect students and myself when systems get it wrong.

I will know I am improving when students who started guarded with adults take more academic and emotional risks, when families feel respected, when I can name moments where policy/data/technology made a student's path more humane, and when my self-talk shifts from "I am failing" to "that did not work; here is my next adjustment."

The reason I started is so it is less likely that another CLD, neurodivergent child slips through cracks that were never accidents.
